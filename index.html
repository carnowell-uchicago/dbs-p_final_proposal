MPCS Wars: The Student Strikes Back

Project proposal · Design, Build, Ship · MPCS 51238 · Autumn 2026

MPCS students need a way to blow off steam during finals week, so this game lets them enjoy a humorous imagining of destroying Crerar Library after hours, when there are no witnesses.

How it works

You start on the 3rd floor of a (loosely recreated) John Crerar Library, armed with a red stapler. Smash windows, knock over chairs, crack tables. Find the fire extinguisher, then use it to break down the door to the main stairwell and head down a floor.

Props don't have fixed point values. Your score comes from how you destroy them: how hard and how squarely you hit, and how completely the pieces separate.

Two leaderboards: fastest time to break out of the floor, and most destruction points.

Floors and walls stay standing until the end. Once you're outside, you attack the front of the building until it starts to collapse.

Users

MPCS students during finals week.

I interviewed three MPCS classmates about how they handle stress and breaks. Names are withheld.

Student 1

"Took time off screens for a few hours (2+) which helped regain sanity."
"How badly each course is scheduled. We can't really have a good break between each."
"Bragging rights with friends for the scoreboard."
"I think that's good for someone who's really pissed off with the course. But for someone who's totally fine with the course it will be harsh"

Student 2

"I grinded through my pain and suffering. I skipped sleep, I skipped another assignment that had late submissions allowed."
"Saw it on an instagram and downloaded and kept playing until phone ran out of charge."
"How difficult the administration tries to make your life."

Student 3

"Went to the gym for a break. Moving and exercise, hitting things oops I mean weights."
"I've been playing multiplayer games like mario kart and smash in the madd center which is in JCL."
"I like this game because I think it will give people common ground even between programs. You need to make it diplomatic so you don't pinpoint certain people."

[Student: rewrite in your words.] What I took from it:

  • All three compete with friends on scores or rankings, which supports the two leaderboards.
  • The frustration is with scheduling and administration, not with people, so the game targets the building, never individuals.
  • Breaks are often physical or off-screen, so the game has to be quick, physical fun to earn its place.

Focus: Systems

I want to explore web based games and physics destruction.
